Understanding Small-Molecule Embeddings: A Comparative Analysis of Molecular Representation Methods

Mondal et al. | ICDCIT | 2027

Abstract

One of the most essential task for computational drug discovery is to learn informative drug representations. Typically, a large set of drug and drug-like molecules are embedded in a latent space, which is then coupled with a predictive model for several downstream tasks or for generating novel molecules. Various molecular representation methods have already been developed ranging from the conventional molecular fingerprint-based methods to generative model-based approaches for representation learning. Based on the choice of learning algorithms, these learned representations can differ substantially. Furthermore, the latent space geometry as well as the string-based representation of a molecule can also have notable effect on the molecular representations. In this study, we performed a comparative analysis between \textit{four} molecular representations methods, including variational autoencoder (VAE), hyperspherical variational autoencoder (S-VAE), ChemBERTa-2, and traditional Morgan fingerprint--based representation. For VAE, both the simplified molecular input line entry system (SMILES) and self-referencing embedded strings (SELFIES) based molecular representation were investigated, whereas the remaining methods were analyzed over only the SMILES representations corresponding to a molecule. Furthermore, the molecular embeddings obtained the representation techniques were also assessed based on molecular reconstruction, preservation of local chemical neighbourhoods, and the organization of molecular properties within the embedding spaces. Overall, this study provides an empirical assessment into how the structural and chemical information is reflected by the learned representations of some commonly used molecular embedding strategies. All the codes will be available at https://github.com/rjdpm/mol-repr.
